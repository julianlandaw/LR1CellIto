APD is the time between upward and downward crossings of −75 mV. Incomplete responses are excluded. An EAD is counted when voltage rises at least 5 mV from a trough at or below −15 mV, at least 100 ms after an upstroke.

X memory and the APD return map

The left panel pairs each complete APD with X at the upstroke. The right panel shows consecutive measured APDs and, for the chaos settings, held-out predictions from a map calibrated on the first 60% of beats. Its inputs are X, APD, and the measured diastolic interval; the open circles need not form a single curve. The dashed diagonal marks unchanged APD.

Calibrated predictions appear after a chaos run with at least 40 complete APDs.

Inspect an action potential

Voltage and currents share the same time axis. The component currents, including transient outward Ito, begin at the upstroke. Net ionic current begins later so the brief sodium spike does not flatten the other traces; it excludes the stimulus. Negative net current drives depolarization. Select a complete APD to inspect its EAD count.

Pacing-cycle-length sweep

Run the current conductance and gate settings independently at each PCL. The scatter shows complete APDs ending during the last paced cycles. The lower plot shows complete APDs per delivered stimulus; values below one indicate missed or incomplete responses. Each point includes 10 conditioning beats before the recorded beats.
